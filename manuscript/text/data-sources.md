## Supplementary methods S4. Data sources and provenance

The three experiments used occurrence evidence from the Atlas of Living Australia (ALA) [@belbin2021ala] for the inclusive period 2001--2025. The infrastructure citation identifies the repository, but it is not a substitute for an occurrence-download DOI. The archived Tasmania and mainland downloads were requested without DOI minting, and the bird workflow retained hashed aggregate-query evidence rather than a DOI-bearing occurrence download. These exact inputs remain traceable within the project through frozen queries, manifests and content hashes, but their absent ALA snapshot DOIs are a publication provenance gap.

| Experiment | Occurrence and authority sources | Main provenance limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Original Tasmania mammals | ALA Tasmania snapshot acquired 24 July 2026 for nine source taxa, of which eight entered the experiment; private camera-survey evidence | No ALA snapshot DOI; camera-data access statement pending author confirmation; legacy predictor lineage described below |
| Mainland mammals transferred to Tasmania | ALA southeastern-mainland and Tasmania snapshot acquired 11 August 2026 for 21 source taxa, with five focal transfer targets; Tasmanian evaluation also used the private camera-survey evidence | No ALA snapshot DOI; camera-data access statement pending author confirmation |
| South-eastern Australian birds | Spatially valid, extant ALA aggregates for 505 queried species, acquired 2 September 2026; Australian Bird Data Version 1 [@garnett2015birddata] and BirdLife Australia Working List v4.3 [@birdlife2023wlab] supplied authority information | Aggregate workflow did not download row-level occurrences or mint an ALA snapshot DOI; BirdLife v4.3 has no DOI or stated machine-readable reuse licence in the retained authority receipt |

The original Tasmania models used a legacy 28-variable stack: 19 bioclimatic variables, elevation, terrain roughness, soil class, distance to freshwater, distance to roads, FPAR, extant vegetation group, secondary land use and human footprint. The archive verifies layer order and grid geometry. Native product versions, units, dates, resampling and nodata rules are unavailable; workbook labels and links are reference descriptions rather than verified construction lineage. FPAR alone has an explicit 2000--2014 reference window. Current environmental-product versions were therefore not assigned retrospectively to this stack.

### Versioned environmental substrate for the mainland and bird experiments

The shared substrate contained 24 variables. Nineteen were WorldClim 2.1 bioclimatic variables at 30 arc-seconds, representing the 1970--2000 climate baseline and accessed through the ALA Web Coverage Service [@fick2017worldclim]. Mean elevation and within-cell sample standard deviation were calculated from the Geoscience Australia SRTM-derived 1 Second Digital Elevation Model v1.0, served through the 2024 WCS endpoint [@gallant2011srtm]. The three categorical variables were the 100-m extant Major Vegetation Group raster from NVIS v7.0 [@dcceew2026nvis], Australian Soil Classification Soil Order from Soil and Landscape Grid of Australia Release 1 collection v4 at 3 arc-seconds [@searle2021soil], and the 250-m Land Use of Australia 2020--21 product (NLUM v7/ALUM v8) [@abares2024landuse]. The source manifest records retrieval on 14 August 2026, source URLs, native resolutions, transformations and SHA-256 hashes; each source was recorded as CC BY 4.0. Later releases were not substituted.

The bird boundary source was the official IBRA v7.1 subregion feature service [@dcceew2026ibra]. The frozen authority check required 419 subregion features mapping to 89 bioregion codes. For every candidate, all features sharing `REG_CODE_7` were dissolved into one complete bioregion geometry. The selected outer units were therefore the complete NCP (Naracoorte Coastal Plain), SEH (South Eastern Highlands) and NNC (NSW North Coast) bioregions, rather than individual subregions. Evaluation used each unbuffered whole bioregion; development excluded all three bioregions and their external 50-km buffers simultaneously.

### Unresolved publication metadata

Three facts require author action before submission: a publication-facing access and permission statement for the private camera-survey source; a decision on how to report the absent ALA snapshot DOIs while preserving the exact retained hashes and queries; and, if exact product citations are required for the original Tasmania predictors, recovery of their native metadata. The available evidence does not support assigning current WorldClim, NVIS, land-use, soil or elevation versions retrospectively to that legacy stack.
